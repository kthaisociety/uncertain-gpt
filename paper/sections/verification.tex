\section{Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

% What the three instruments of Section~\ref{sec:suite} returned for the change
% measured here, weakest first, and what the identity assertion does NOT
% certify. The restraint in the last paragraph is load-bearing: the identity
% was passed at 1e-15 by two deliberately broken inputs.

Folding is a claim about an implementation, so the numbers of
Section~\ref{sec:find:fusion} rest on gates a wrong fold fails. Evaluation at
$S = 32$ on validation and test is byte-identical before and after on all four arms,
and is the weakest of the three: it draws with the unfused sample kernels, none of
which folding touches, so a training path could be arbitrarily wrong and this would
still pass. What it establishes is that the inference path and the
checkpoints were not disturbed. Running each fused kernel and the kernels it replaces
on identical inputs leaves mean-field and Vadam bit-identical on every output buffer
and the low-rank arm within $1.00$ ULP, scored at the larger of a value's own
magnitude and the buffer's RMS; rebuilding with multiply-add contraction disabled
makes all three bit-identical, which puts the residual in contraction rather than in
the arithmetic. And $100$ steps of normal-mode training against the pre-folding source
reproduce every printed step line on all four arms, including both summary lines of
the control, whose in-training output is the reference the repository is calibrated
against.

The identity of Equation~\ref{eq:decomp} is asserted at $10^{-5}$ in double precision
on every evaluation and the process exits nonzero on a violation; the worst residual
behind any number in this paper is $4.4 \times 10^{-15}$. It certifies less than that
suggests. Both halves of the right-hand side are computed directly rather than one
from the other, so a violation is an error in the accumulation. But the identity is
normalisation-free, since $\bar{p}$ is the mean of the very vectors it is checked
against, and it therefore holds for any non-negative vectors: an unnormalised
probability row and a stale sample buffer were both injected into the harness and both
passed at $10^{-15}$ while the metrics printed beside them were wrong. What it tests
is the fold arithmetic and not the probabilities that entered it. A cheaper selftest
reaches the same arithmetic first, at $10^{-12}$, but only at $V = 2$ and $S \leq 2$.
