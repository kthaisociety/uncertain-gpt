No arm here is limited by the arithmetic of its posterior. Every parameter-sized
region in Table~\ref{tab:bandwidth} runs within a few percent of every other one per
buffer walked, so a strategy's per-step training cost on this card is its touch count
times $44.2$\,MB divided by the bandwidth its kernels achieve: about $240$\,GB/s for
a sequence walking three or four buffers each, about $190$ for one kernel holding
eight. That estimate is available from a method's definition, by counting the buffers
it keeps and the reads and writes a step makes of each, less the eight touches a
deterministic AdamW step was paying anyway.

The same count says which strategies a larger batch cannot rescue, since amortisation
followed from the numerator being fixed, and it is fixed because the state is
proportional to the parameter count and nothing else. A covariance factor of rank $r$
multiplies that term by $r$, which is measured here: the low-rank arm still pays
$11.65\%$ of a step at $B = 128$. A per-example perturbation of the kind
\citet{wen2018flipout} move into the activations puts the sampling cost in the $O(B
\cdot T)$ term instead, where it grows with the batch as the network does and its share
does not fall; that follows from the mechanism and was not measured here. A covariance
fitted after training \citep{ritter2018scalable,daxberger2021laplace} or collected from
the trajectory \citep{maddox2019swag} moves the traffic out of the step, and MC dropout
\citep{gal2016dropout} never allocates any: all three pay at evaluation.

Where folding stops paying is also a count. Two of the three Bayesian arms reach the
floor their state implies, and the gap between the traffic removed and the time
recovered is bandwidth a wider kernel gives up, which grows with the live state it
holds. The low-rank arm's remaining region is not a bandwidth problem at all: its KL
setup blocks on host round-trips and small per-layer cuBLAS calls. Sweeping $T$ as well
as $B$ would separate a dependence on tokens per step from one on batch size, and like
everything above it runs from the checkpoints and logs already in the repository.
