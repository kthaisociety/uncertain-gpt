\label{sec:findings}

% folding returns the redundant traffic and nothing else, Vadam's fitted
% posterior is its prior, and an epistemic term depends on the S it was read

\subsection{A posterior's per-step cost does not contain the batch size}
\label{sec:find:flat}

Each arm's posterior work costs the same milliseconds at $64$ tokens per step as at
$16{,}384$: across the $256$-fold sweep the directly timed Bayesian regions hold to
within $1.5\%$ of themselves on every arm (Table~\ref{tab:posterior}), the width of
the measurement and not a trend in it. Nothing in those regions has a token in it.

The network's cost grows with tokens and the posterior's does not, so the
posterior's share of a step falls, at a local exponent of $1.005$ for mean-field
over the last two points. That asymptote is not the whole curve: between $B = 1$ and
$B = 2$ the exponent is $0.148$ for mean-field and $0.196$ for Vadam, because the
network is not $O(B \cdot T)$ at the bottom of the sweep either, twice the tokens
costing the control arm a factor of $1.16$ there and a factor of two from $B = 16$
up (Table~\ref{tab:amortization}). Nor is the fixed numerator a property of
posteriors: the control's own parameter-sized share falls just as far.

What fails to amortise is state not proportional to the parameter count, which is
why the low-rank arm still owns $11.65\%$ of a step at its largest measurable batch
where mean-field owns $0.75\%$ and Vadam $0.31\%$. All of it is per-step cost,
pricing neither the ensemble's training runs nor a variational arm's evaluation
passes.

\subsection{Folding returns the redundant traffic and nothing else}
\label{sec:find:fusion}

Folding an arm's parameter-sized passes into one kernel buys time in proportion to the
traffic it deletes. Mean-field's $32$ touches drop to $15$ for $1.76\times$ of $O(P)$
time at $B = 1$, the low-rank arm's $122$ per layer to $58$ for $1.14$ to
$1.16\times$, and Vadam's $11$ to $9$ for between $0.98$ and $1.04\times$
(Table~\ref{tab:fusion}). The control's code is unchanged in both sweeps and comes out
within $1\%$ of itself, so that band is drift, and Vadam's result sits inside it.

Vadam's pass count predicted that null before the sweep ran. Two touches of slack
against a floor of $9$ is $1.22\times$ of traffic, and a fused kernel gives back
bandwidth: mean-field's streams eight buffers at once where the sequence it replaced
walked three or four each (Table~\ref{tab:bandwidth}). At that discount a $2.13\times$
traffic cut is still $1.76\times$ of time and a $1.22\times$ cut is nothing.

What folding returns is a fixed count of buffer touches, so it amortises exactly as
the posterior's own cost does: the same $1.76\times$ is a fifth of a mean-field step
at $B = 1$ and under a percent of one at $B = 128$ (Table~\ref{tab:crossover}). Taking
the boundary per arm at the $1\%$ the control reproduces itself in, mean-field crosses
at $B = 128$, Vadam is inside that band throughout, and the low-rank arm is outside it
wherever this card can run.

\subsection{Vadam's fitted posterior is its prior}
\label{sec:find:vadam}

What the arms return at a fixed budget of $100{,}000$ steps is read from their
checkpoints, on both splits with neither preferred. The ensemble has the best mixture
NLPD and the worst ECE by close to a factor of two and Vadam holds the opposite corner
(Table~\ref{tab:quality}), with mean-field and the low-rank arm between them, no
ranking being claimed on the NLPD column: with one run per arm, nothing here measures
the noise floor its spread would have to clear.

A larger epistemic term is not a better posterior. Vadam's in-distribution epistemic
is $0.0381$ nats on validation against mean-field's $0.0108$
(Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty}), more than three times it, while its separation between
in- and off-distribution input sits with the other variational arms at $1.197$
(Table~\ref{tab:ood}). The spread is the prior's. Equation~\ref{eq:vadam-sigma} weighs
the data's contribution to the precision, $N s$, against $\lambda = 2500$, so the
per-token RMS gradient would have to reach $\sqrt{\lambda / N} = 5.27 \times 10^{-3}$
where the median observed value is $2.71 \times 10^{-5}$, short by
$195\times$.\footnote{Read from the trained checkpoint by
\texttt{dev/vadam\_posterior.py}, the one measurement here taken from a checkpoint
rather than a logged run.} At that median the data supplies $0.0026\%$ of the
precision and $99.8\%$ of parameters sit within $10\%$ of the prior standard
deviation; the minibatch bias of that paper's own Theorem~1 accounts for at most
$16\times$ of the shortfall on the gradient scale, and correcting for it in full moves
that $99.8\%$ to $85.1\%$.

The mean did fit: $\mu$ ends at correlation $0.083$ with its initialisation, having
moved $1.27$ times its own initial norm. What the width reports is the threshold
$\lambda = 1/\sigma_{\text{init}}^{2}$ sets against this model's gradients, not a run
that failed to train, and noise that does not shrink as the mean fits is why that arm
fails the one-batch memorisation gate.

\subsection{An epistemic term is a Monte Carlo estimate at some $S$}
\label{sec:find:s}

Every uncertainty number above carries its $S$: the estimator averages over $S$ draws,
is exactly zero at $S = 1$ by construction, one sample disagreeing with nothing, and
approaches its converged value from below (Figure~\ref{fig:s-convergence}). Where it
converges is a property of the arm rather than of the harness: mean-field is flat by
$S = 32$ and confirmed flat to $S = 128$, though its ECE rises at every doubling over
that range while its mixture NLPD improves. Vadam and the low-rank arm are still
climbing at $S = 32$ and flatten only at $S = 64$, which is why
Section~\ref{sec:suite}'s convention sits there rather than where mean-field alone
would put it.

The ensemble cannot be read at an $S$ of anyone's choosing. Its $s$-th sample is its
$s$-th trained member, so $S$ is capped at $E = 5$ and a further draw costs a training
run rather than a forward pass. Tables~\ref{tab:uncertainty} and \ref{tab:ood} state
it at $S = 5$ beside variational arms at $S = 64$, and every epistemic term compared
across those rows carries that difference.
