A training step's cost is a property of an implementation as much as of a method.
Two Bayesian methods timed in two codebases differ in their kernels, their data path,
their optimiser constants and their precision, and a gap in step time between them is
attributable to no one of those.

uncertain-gpt holds all of them fixed. One CUDA implementation of a GPT-2
\citep{radford2019language} supplies the kernels, the forward and backward pass,
the dataloader, the checkpoint format, the timing harness and the evaluation
harness. A method enters as an update kernel and a callback that returns posterior
draw $s$, and inherits the rest, so two arms differ only in what they store and how
many passes they make over that store. Sharing kernels requires owning them: a
framework supplies the optimiser, the allocator and the choice of kernel, which are
the terms a shared implementation exists to remove, so the network is written from
scratch in C and CUDA and every kernel outside cuBLAS is checked against a
single-threaded C reference.

The four arms implemented against that shared code are methods from published work
rather than stand-ins for them: Bayes-by-Backprop \citep{blundell2015weight}, Vadam
\citep{khan2018vadam}, a learned low-rank-plus-diagonal Gaussian covariance, and a
five-member deep ensemble \citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple} as the control, which
stores no posterior and therefore prices what the other three add.

Section~\ref{sec:findings} reports four measurements on them. Two are readings a
count of an arm's buffers implies and the suite confirms: a posterior's per-step cost
is flat to within $1.5\%$ across a $256$-fold batch sweep, and folding an arm's
parameter-sized passes into one kernel returns time in proportion to the traffic it
deletes, which for Vadam is nothing. Two are properties of the posteriors themselves:
Vadam's fitted posterior at this scale is the prior it started from, and an epistemic
term is biased low until its sample count converges, at an $S$ that differs by arm.
