\label{sec:quality}

The ensemble has the best mixture NLPD of the four and the worst ECE, by close
to a factor of two; Vadam holds the opposite corner. Mean-field and the
low-rank arm sit between them, separated by less than the two splits disagree
about them, so those two are not ranked. Every claim here is read off
\texttt{test}, and validation agrees on each one except the middle two arms'
calibration ordering, which reverses between splits.

A larger epistemic term is not a better posterior. Vadam's in-distribution
epistemic is more than three times mean-field's while its separation ratio sits
with the other variational arms, far below the ensemble's. That spread is prior
noise rather than learned disagreement, for the reason below.

\paragraph{Why $S = 32$.}
Epistemic uncertainty is a Monte Carlo estimate over $S$ draws, biased low
until $S$ converges (Figure~\ref{fig:s-convergence}). Mean-field is flat by
$S = 32$ and confirmed flat out to $S = 128$. Under-sampling flatters the
variational arms: from $S = 8$ to $S = 32$ their in-distribution epistemic
grows faster than their off-distribution epistemic, so their separation ratios
\emph{fall} while the ensemble stays pinned at $S = E$. Converging $S$ made the
headline gap larger, not smaller.

The low-rank arm gains a further few percent of epistemic between $S = 32$ and
$S = 64$ and only then stops, so at $S = 32$ mean-field sits at its limit and
the low-rank arm slightly below its own. That asymmetry favours mean-field in a
head-to-head and is small against the gaps this section reads. Moving the
convention to $S = 64$ would remove it and costs no retraining.

\paragraph{Calibration degrades with $S$, in one arm.}
Mean-field's ECE rises at every doubling of $S$ from $2$ to $128$ while its
mixture NLPD improves, with no retraining between the two ends. The low-rank
arm does not share the pattern, which argues against a binning artifact: if the
estimator or the bin count were responsible, both arms would show it. It was
not chased further. Any ECE quoted for mean-field carries its $S$ or means
nothing.

\paragraph{Vadam's posterior is the prior, and that is structural.}
The arm's mean left initialisation almost completely and its training loss fell
like the others'. Its posterior \emph{scale} did not move.
Equation~\ref{eq:vadam-sigma} makes the data's contribution to the precision
$N s$, weighed against $\lambda = 2500$, so for the data to matter the
per-token RMS gradient has to reach $\sqrt{\lambda / N} = 5.27 \times 10^{-3}$.
The median observed value is $2.71 \times 10^{-5}$, short by a factor of
$195$,\footnote{Read from the trained checkpoint by
\texttt{dev/vadam\_posterior.py}, the one measurement in this paper taken from
a checkpoint rather than a logged run; the rest of the arithmetic is $\lambda$
and $N$.} and the minibatch bias in \citeauthor{khan2018vadam}'s own Theorem~1
accounts for at most a factor of $16$ of that at this batch size. The posterior
sits at the prior in almost every coordinate, and the arm reports the prior's
spread as epistemic uncertainty.

The kernels, the sampler and the transcription were checked before this was
concluded (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}), and rerunning the arm reproduces it.
The knob that would change it is $\lambda$, and choosing $\lambda$ by anything
other than the initialisation is a modelling decision this comparison does not
get to make.
