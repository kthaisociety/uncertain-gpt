\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Variational Bayesian neural networks.}
\citet{hinton1993mdl} priced weight uncertainty as description length and
gave the objective its first form. \citet{graves2011practical} made it run
on real networks. \citet{blundell2015weight} put the reparameterisation of
\citet{kingma2014autoencoding} inside it, which turned the variational
parameters into ordinary backprop targets and produced Bayes-by-Backprop as
it is used today. \citet{wen2018flipout} attacked the cost of drawing an
independent perturbation per example, which a shared per-step draw does not
pay but a large-batch implementation eventually must. The thread establishes
the estimator; it does not price it against alternatives running on shared
kernels. Our Bayes-by-Backprop arm is \citeauthor{blundell2015weight}'s
method unchanged, and what this paper adds is that its buffer count and its
step time sit beside three alternatives measured on the same device.

\paragraph{Optimiser-shaped variational inference.}
\citet{khan2018vadam} observed that Adam's second moment already estimates a
curvature, so it can double as a posterior precision and the posterior costs
almost nothing beyond the optimiser state. \citet{osawa2019practical} scaled
natural-gradient variational inference to ImageNet, and \citet{shen2024ivon}
carried the same idea to GPT-2-scale training. The thread's claim is that a
posterior can be nearly free. We turn it into a measurement: the
optimiser-shaped arm runs on the same kernels as an explicit-variance arm and
an ensemble, so its step time and its VRAM are directly comparable to theirs.

\paragraph{Post-hoc and covariance-structured posteriors.}
This is the closest existing work to the low-rank arm. \citet{ritter2018scalable}
fit a Kronecker-factored Laplace approximation to the curvature at a trained
point, giving a structured covariance without changing the training run.
\citet{daxberger2021laplace} turned that into a default recipe --- fit the
Laplace approximation after the fact, often on the last layer only, and pay
nothing during training. \citet{maddox2019swag} build exactly the covariance
this paper's third arm uses, a low-rank factor plus a diagonal, but collect
the factor from the SGD trajectory once training is over.

All three are post-hoc by construction. The factor is read off a completed
run, so the training loop is untouched and the cost lands at the end. Our
low-rank arm inverts that: the rank-$r$ factor $A$ is a variational parameter
updated every step by backprop through the sample, so the cost lands in the
training loop, where it can be measured per step against mean-field and
against an ensemble. The posterior is then fit by the same objective that
fits the weights, rather than by a curvature estimate at a point, and the KL
term has to be evaluated without ever forming the $P \times P$ covariance ---
the identities in Section~\ref{sec:m:lowrank}. What we give up relative to
the post-hoc line is
its main selling point: those methods can be applied to a model somebody else
trained, and this one cannot.

\paragraph{Baselines that win on cost.}
Deep ensembles \citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple} need no posterior machinery
at all and match or beat variational methods on predictive uncertainty
across the benchmarks of \citet{ovadia2019trust}, at $E$ times the parameters
and $E$ times the training. That is
why an ensemble is the control arm here and not a footnote: it is the thing
the other three have to justify themselves against, and it is trained by the
same code they are. MC dropout \citep{gal2016dropout} is the other cheap
baseline. The network in this paper has no dropout layers, so it is out of
scope rather than omitted for a reason.

\paragraph{Evaluation vocabulary.}
We report calibration as expected calibration error \citep{guo2017calibration}
and split predictive uncertainty into aleatoric and epistemic parts in the
sense of \citet{kendall2017uncertainties}. \citet{ovadia2019trust} is the
reference for how such methods behave under dataset shift and is the standard
this paper does not meet: our off-distribution stream is uniform-random
tokens, which tests the direction of the epistemic term and nothing about
shifted text (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
