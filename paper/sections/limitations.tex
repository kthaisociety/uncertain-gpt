\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

% The same facts as before, reframed for a mechanism claim: what a cost result at
% this scale does and does not transfer, what the thermal fault bounds, and which
% comparisons are confounded.

\paragraph{The scale bounds the milliseconds, not the counts.}
$11{,}061{,}531$ parameters on a $6$\,GB card, picked so all four arms fit at once.
Touch counts do not depend on $P$, and neither does the flat numerator of
Section~\ref{sec:cost:flat}, but every millisecond and every GB/s here belongs to this
card. Where the network becomes token-bound does depend on the model: a larger one
saturates the card at a smaller $B$ and reaches the $1/B$ regime sooner. The vocabulary
of $27$ keeps the token embedding negligible, so nothing covers a posterior over a
large embedding table.

\paragraph{The measurement is bounded by the card and by one axis.}
The card throttles, so the two sweeps are comparable only to about $1\%$: with
per-point cooldown the later one still started a few degrees warmer and the same region
comes out a few percent slower for it. That is what the unchanged control arm is for,
and no claim in Section~\ref{sec:cost:fusion} rests on a difference smaller than the
drift measured on it. The low-rank arm at $B = 256$ allocated with $248$\,MB of
$6144$\,MB free and then ran at about four times what its own curve predicts, because
past a couple of hundred megabytes of headroom the driver backs allocations with host
memory and the step time stops being a property of the kernels; on WSL2
\texttt{cudaMalloc} succeeds in that state, so the guard is a headroom floor and that
arm's sweep ends at $B = 128$. The sweep also varies $B$ at $T = 64$ throughout, so
what is measured is a dependence on batch size and not on tokens per step. One training
run per arm, no seed sweep, no error bars, and three backward kernels accumulate with
\texttt{atomicAdd}, so training is not bit-reproducible in principle; normal-mode runs
at this configuration do reproduce, which is what the trajectory check of
Section~\ref{sec:quality} uses, and one-batch overfit runs do not.

\paragraph{The quality comparison is confounded and unconverged.}
$100{,}000$ steps at $256$ tokens per step is $28\%$ of one epoch, so every quality
number is what a method does at a fixed step budget. Loss tracks the injected noise
scale monotonically across arms, so an arm that samples less aggressively looks better
on predictive metrics for a reason unrelated to its posterior, and the
off-distribution stream is uniform-random token ids rather than shifted text, which
supports no comparison with the shift literature \citep{ovadia2019trust}. The low-rank
arm is Bayesian over $96\%$ of the model and the ensemble evaluates at $S = E = 5$, so
a mixture metric read across arms compares different coverage and different sample
counts.
