\section{What the posterior costs}
\label{sec:cost}

% Three results, in this order: the posterior's cost is fixed per step, its share
% therefore falls (with the small-B caveat in the main text, not a footnote), and
% the part of it that is redundant traffic is removable. Interpret the tables; do
% not retype them.

\subsection{The cost is fixed per step}
\label{sec:cost:flat}

Each arm's posterior work costs the same milliseconds at $64$ tokens per step as at
$16{,}384$. Across a $256$-fold batch sweep the directly timed Bayesian regions hold
to within $1.1\%$ of themselves for mean-field, $1.5\%$ for Vadam and $1.4\%$ for
the low-rank arm (Table~\ref{tab:posterior}): the width of the measurement, not a
trend in it.

Nothing in those regions has a token in it. They stream parameter-sized buffers, and
how many tokens produced the gradient in one does not change its length. The traffic
is what the time is: every parameter-sized region of the mean-field step lands
between $234.7$ and $253.7$\,GB/s and their aggregate at $240.8$\,GB/s
(Table~\ref{tab:bandwidth}), so regions sharing no arithmetic differ only in how
many buffers each walks.

\subsection{Amortisation, and where it stops}
\label{sec:cost:amort}

The network's cost grows with tokens and the posterior's does not, so the
posterior's share of a step falls: mean-field from $34.40\%$ at $B = 1$ to $0.75\%$
at $B = 256$, at a local exponent of $1.005$ over the last two points, Vadam at
$0.998$ (Table~\ref{tab:posterior}).

That asymptote is not the whole curve. Between $B = 1$ and $B = 2$ the exponent is
$0.148$ for mean-field and $0.196$ for Vadam, and the reason is the denominator: the
network is not $O(B \cdot T)$ at the bottom of the sweep either. The control arm
shows that with no posterior in the way, costing $8.45$\,ms at $64$ tokens and
$9.83$\,ms at $128$, a factor of $1.16$ for twice the work, while from $B = 16$
upward each doubling of tokens doubles the step (Table~\ref{tab:amortization}).
Below it a forward and backward pass costs more in launches than in tokens, so
$1/B$ is the asymptote and not the curve.

The fixed numerator is not a property of posteriors either: the control's own
parameter-sized work falls from $17.7\%$ of its step to $0.5\%$ over the same sweep.
What fails to amortise is state not proportional to the parameter count, which is why
the low-rank arm still owns $11.65\%$ of a step at $B = 128$, at an exponent of $0.824$.
And all of it is per-step cost, so a sweep over $B$ prices neither the ensemble's $E$
runs nor a variational arm's $S$ passes at evaluation.

\subsection{The removable part is the redundant traffic}
\label{sec:cost:fusion}

Folding an arm's parameter-sized passes into one kernel buys time in proportion to the
traffic it deletes, and nothing where there is none to delete. Mean-field drops $32$
touches to $15$ and its $O(P)$ time by $1.76\times$ at $B = 1$, falling to $1.65\times$
at $B = 128$; the low-rank arm drops $122$ units per layer to $58$ and gains $1.14$ to
$1.16\times$; Vadam drops $11$ to $9$ and lands between $0.98$ and $1.04\times$
(Table~\ref{tab:fusion}). The control is unchanged code measured in both sweeps and
comes out within $1\%$ of itself, so anything inside that band is drift, which is where
Vadam's sits.

That null is what Vadam's pass count predicts. Two touches of slack against a floor
of $9$ is $1.22\times$ of traffic, and a fused kernel gives back bandwidth:
mean-field's streams eight buffers at once at $192.9$\,GB/s where the sequence it
replaced walked three or four each at $240.8$\,GB/s. At that discount a $2.13\times$
traffic cut is still $1.76\times$ of time and a $1.22\times$ cut is nothing, so a pass
reduction quoted without a timing would have reported that arm as a $1.22\times$
improvement.

Folding moves the amortisation curve rather than bending it: mean-field's
parameter-sized share after folding is $0.68$ of its share before at $B = 1$ and $0.60$
at $B = 128$. Batching moves an arm along the curve, folding moves the curve down, and
folding therefore returns most where amortisation has not arrived.
