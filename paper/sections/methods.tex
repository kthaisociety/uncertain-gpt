\section{Four posteriors over one network}
\label{sec:methods}

% What is held fixed lives in the introduction and Section~\ref{sec:setup};
% do not state it a third time here. Each subsection says only how weights
% are sampled and how they are updated.

The shared header holds the kernels, buffer layout, forward and backward pass,
dataloader, checkpoint format and evaluation harness. Each arm's source file
holds only its optimiser.

\subsection{Bayes-by-Backprop}
\label{sec:m:bbb}
% Mean-field Gaussian posterior, reparameterised sample per step, ELBO with
% the KL weight fixed by the data (1/N), not tuned.

A fully factorised Gaussian posterior over every parameter. Each carries a mean
$\mu_i$ and an unconstrained $\rho_i$, and a step draws
\begin{equation}
  w_i = \mu_i + \mathrm{softplus}(\rho_i)\,\varepsilon_i ,
  \qquad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) ,
\end{equation}
one draw shared by the whole batch, reparameterised so the gradient reaches
both moments \citep{kingma2014autoencoding,blundell2015weight}. The softplus
keeps the standard deviation positive under AdamW's scale-free step at an
initialisation near $10^{-3}$. The prior is a zero-mean Gaussian of standard
deviation $0.1$ with a closed-form KL gradient. The objective is the ELBO,
$\mathbb{E}_q[\mathrm{NLL}] + \mathrm{KL}(q\|p)/N$, with $N$ the training token
count read off the loader rather than a tuned weight; a stale value from a
different corpus cost about a nat of NLL.

This is the largest fixed footprint of the four: ten parameter-sized buffers
against four for a deterministic model ($\mu$, $\rho$, an AdamW moment pair for
each, the sampled weights and their gradient, and an accumulator per moment),
and two AdamW passes per step. Regenerating $\varepsilon$ from the index
removes an eleventh buffer without changing the arithmetic, and the one-batch
overfit test reaches the same loss either way.

\subsection{Vadam}
\label{sec:m:vadam}
% The second moment doubles as the posterior precision, so the posterior is
% (almost) free. Note where N enters the scale.

Adam's second moment already estimates a curvature, so \citet{khan2018vadam}
reuse it as the posterior precision. The posterior is a diagonal Gaussian whose
mean alone is free, and the scale is read off the optimiser,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N s_i + \lambda}} ,
  \qquad
  w_i = \mu_i + \sigma_i \varepsilon_i ,
  \label{eq:vadam-sigma}
\end{equation}
with $s$ the second moment, $N$ the training token count and $\lambda$ the
prior precision. Figure~1 of \citet{khan2018vadam}, transcribed unchanged:
\begin{align}
  m &\leftarrow \gamma_1 m + (1 - \gamma_1)\left(g + \tfrac{\lambda}{N}\mu\right) , \\
  s &\leftarrow \gamma_2 s + (1 - \gamma_2)\, g \circ g , \\
  \mu &\leftarrow \mu - \alpha\, \hat{m} \big/ \left(\sqrt{\hat{s}} + \tfrac{\lambda}{N}\right) ,
\end{align}
with $\hat{m}, \hat{s}$ the bias-corrected moments and $g$ the gradient at the
sampled weights. That is one Adam step with the prior folded into the gradient
and $\lambda/N$ in place of Adam's $\epsilon$, and nothing beyond Adam's own
$(m, s)$ is allocated.

The square root appears in the $\mu$ denominator but not in
Equation~\ref{eq:vadam-sigma}, where $\sigma$ takes $s$ raw. Footnote~2 of
\citet{khan2018vadam} states that it ``does not affect a fixed point but might
still affect the steps taken''; that asymmetry is kept.

$\lambda$ is derived, not tuned: matching the initialisation to the prior gives
$\lambda = 1/\sigma_{\text{init}}^{2} = 2500$ and a prior standard deviation of
$0.02$. The data's contribution to the precision is $N s$ against $\lambda$, so
small gradients leave the posterior at the prior rather than merely slowing it
(Section~\ref{sec:quality}). $N$ belongs to the training run, so evaluation on
validation or test reads it from the training split.

\subsection{Low-rank plus diagonal covariance}
\label{sec:m:lowrank}
% Posterior covariance as a rank-r factor plus a diagonal: what the extra
% rank buys, and what it costs in memory and per-step time.

This arm replaces mean-field independence with a limited amount of
correlation,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma = A A^{\top} + \mathrm{diag}(d^{\,2}) ,
  \qquad A \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times r},\; d \in \mathbb{R}^{P},\; r = 8 .
\end{equation}
$\Sigma$ is $P \times P$ and is never formed; sampling uses the factors,
\begin{equation}
  w_i = \mu_i + \sum_{k<r} A_{ik} z_k + d_i \varepsilon_i ,
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon$ drawn per weight from the index-keyed Philox stream and one
$z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_r)$ per weight matrix per forward pass, shared by all
$P$ of its weights. That shared draw is the coupling.

$d = \mathrm{softplus}(\rho_d)$. $A$ is signed, so softplus cannot bound it and
it takes its own learning rate, $10^{-5}$ against $10^{-4}$ for the means. The
prior is $\mathcal{N}(0, I/\lambda)$ with $\lambda = 100$, matching the
mean-field prior standard deviation of $0.1$, and its KL keeps $\Sigma$
factored through
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{tr}(\Sigma) = \|A\|_F^2 + \|d\|^2 , \qquad
  \log\det(\Sigma) = \log\det(M) + \textstyle\sum_i \log d_i^2 , \qquad
  M = I_r + A^{\top} D^{-1} A ,
\end{equation}
which turns a $P \times P$ determinant into an $r \times r$ one. The gradients
follow from the Woodbury identity and reduce to mean-field at $A = 0$, where
the diagonal's stationary point is $1/\sqrt{\lambda}$.

The posterior covers the four GEMM weight matrices per block ($W_{qkv}$,
$W_{proj}$, $W_{fc}$, $W_{fc2}$), or $10{,}616{,}832$ of the model's
$11{,}061{,}531$ parameters, each with its own $A$, $d$ and $z$, so nothing is
correlated across matrices or blocks. Sampling is a separate kernel: the
network calls cuBLAS, so there is no GEMM here to fuse into. The rank is
compile-time so the length-$r$ loops unroll, and a checkpoint is evaluated at
the rank it was trained at.

\subsection{Deep ensemble (control)}
\label{sec:m:ensemble}
% The control arm: no posterior machinery at all, E independently trained
% members, and the reference the other three are measured against.

$E = 5$ independently trained deterministic models
\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, differing only in the initialisation seed.
The network has no dropout and the dataloader is sequential, so initialisation
is the only source of diversity, and there is no bagging. Member probabilities
are averaged at inference, never logits.

This arm introduces no kernels: members train through the shared forward and
backward pass and the shared AdamW, and its evaluation callback returns a
pointer to member $s$. It is the same network with the Bayesian machinery
deleted, so every buffer and kernel pass the other three add is attributable to
their posterior. A Bayesian arm buys its $(S+1)$-th sample for one forward
pass; this arm's $S$ is capped at the members it paid to train.
