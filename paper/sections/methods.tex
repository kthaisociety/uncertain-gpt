\section{Four posteriors over one network}
\label{sec:methods}

% The framing that makes the comparison controlled: state once, here, what
% is held fixed (architecture, data, forward and backward pass, kernels,
% RNG discipline) so each subsection below only has to say how weights are
% sampled and how they are updated.

The four arms share one implementation of the network. Kernels, buffer
layout, forward pass, backward pass, dataloader, checkpoint format, and the
evaluation harness live in a single header; each arm's own source file holds
its optimiser and nothing else. A fix to the network lands in all four arms
in one edit, and the buffer layout is computed in one place, so an arm cannot
quietly disagree with another about where a tensor is.

Held fixed across arms: the architecture and parameter layout, the forward
and backward kernels including the cuBLAS calls, the dataloader and the order
it emits batches in, the AdamW constants of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, fp32
precision, and the RNG discipline --- perturbations are a pure function of
(seed, step, index), regenerated in the backward pass instead of stored, and
evaluation draws from a stream no training step consumed.

Two things vary: how an arm produces a weight vector for a step, and how it
turns the gradient with respect to that vector into a parameter update. The
subsections below say only that. Everything a subsection does not mention is
the shared network.

\subsection{Bayes-by-Backprop}
\label{sec:m:bbb}
% Mean-field Gaussian posterior, reparameterised sample per step, ELBO with
% the KL weight fixed by the data (1/N), not tuned.

A fully factorised Gaussian posterior over every parameter, with both moments
free. Each parameter carries a mean $\mu_i$ and an unconstrained
$\rho_i$, and a step draws
\begin{equation}
  w_i = \mu_i + \mathrm{softplus}(\rho_i)\,\varepsilon_i ,
  \qquad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) ,
\end{equation}
one draw shared by the whole batch, reparameterised so that the gradient
flows to both moments \citep{kingma2014autoencoding,blundell2015weight}. The
softplus keeps the standard deviation positive and compresses its update:
AdamW's step is about the learning rate whatever the gradient's magnitude, so
an unconstrained standard deviation initialised near $10^{-3}$ would move by
its own size every step. The prior is a zero-mean Gaussian of standard
deviation $0.1$, entering through a closed-form KL gradient rather than a
sampled estimate.

The objective is the ELBO, $\mathbb{E}_q[\mathrm{NLL}] + \mathrm{KL}(q\|p)/N$.
The $1/N$ is not a tuning knob. The KL is a per-dataset quantity while the
reported NLL is a per-token mean, so $N$ is the training token count and the
weight is read off the loader at startup. Fixing this rather than tuning it
mattered: a stale hand-set value carried over from a different corpus cost
about a nat of NLL and made the Bayesian arms look categorically worse than
they are.

This is the arm with the largest fixed footprint. It needs ten
parameter-sized buffers where a deterministic model needs four --- $\mu$ and
$\rho$, an AdamW moment pair for each, the sampled weights and their
gradient, and an accumulator for each moment's gradient --- and it runs two
AdamW passes per step. Regenerating $\varepsilon$ from the index removes an
eleventh buffer and its traffic while changing no arithmetic; the one-batch
overfit test reaches the same loss either way.

\subsection{Vadam}
\label{sec:m:vadam}
% Variational online Newton read as a perturbation of Adam: the second
% moment doubles as the posterior precision, so the posterior is (almost)
% free. Note where N enters the scale.

Adam's second moment already estimates a curvature, so \citet{khan2018vadam}
reuse it as the posterior precision instead of learning a second set of
variance parameters. The posterior is again a diagonal Gaussian, but only its
mean is a free parameter: the scale is read off the optimiser,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N s_i + \lambda}} ,
  \qquad
  w_i = \mu_i + \sigma_i \varepsilon_i ,
  \label{eq:vadam-sigma}
\end{equation}
with $s$ the second moment, $N$ the training token count and $\lambda$ the
prior precision. We transcribe Figure~1 of \citet{khan2018vadam} unchanged:
\begin{align}
  m &\leftarrow \gamma_1 m + (1 - \gamma_1)\left(g + \tfrac{\lambda}{N}\mu\right) , \\
  s &\leftarrow \gamma_2 s + (1 - \gamma_2)\, g \circ g , \\
  \mu &\leftarrow \mu - \alpha\, \hat{m} \big/ \left(\sqrt{\hat{s}} + \tfrac{\lambda}{N}\right) ,
\end{align}
with $\hat{m}, \hat{s}$ the bias-corrected moments and $g$ the gradient at the
sampled weights. That is one Adam step with the prior folded into the gradient
and $\lambda/N$ in place of Adam's $\epsilon$. Nothing beyond Adam's own
$(m, s)$ is allocated, which is the arm's entire claim on cost.

Two details are load-bearing and neither is a free choice.

The square root appears in the $\mu$ denominator and not in
Equation~\ref{eq:vadam-sigma}, where $\sigma$ takes $s$ raw. It reads like a
transcription error and is not: footnote~2 of \citet{khan2018vadam} states
that the square root ``does not affect a fixed point but might still affect
the steps taken''. The asymmetry is the paper's, and we kept it, because
matching a published method matters more here than making it internally
tidy.

$\lambda$ is derived, not tuned. Matching the initialisation to the prior it
is drawn from gives $\lambda = 1/\sigma_{\text{init}}^{2} = 2500$, which also
fixes the prior standard deviation at $0.02$. And $N$ sits inside the
posterior rather than beside it: what the data contributes to the precision
in Equation~\ref{eq:vadam-sigma} is $N s$, measured against $\lambda$. Two
consequences follow. Small gradients do not merely shrink the posterior's
movement, they leave it at the prior --- Section~\ref{sec:quality} reports
where this arm landed. And $N$ is a property of the training run, not of the
data in front of the model, so evaluating on validation or test still reads
$N$ from the training split; taking it from the split being evaluated would
silently rescale every posterior sample.

\subsection{Low-rank plus diagonal covariance}
\label{sec:m:lowrank}
% Posterior covariance as a rank-r factor plus a diagonal: what the extra
% rank is meant to buy, and what it costs in memory and per-step time.

Mean-field assumes every weight is independent of every other. This arm buys
back a limited amount of correlation with a covariance of the form
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma = A A^{\top} + \mathrm{diag}(d^{\,2}) ,
  \qquad A \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times r},\; d \in \mathbb{R}^{P},\; r = 8 .
\end{equation}
$\Sigma$ is $P \times P$ and is never formed. Sampling uses the factors
directly,
\begin{equation}
  w_i = \mu_i + \sum_{k<r} A_{ik} z_k + d_i \varepsilon_i ,
\end{equation}
with one $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I_r)$ drawn per weight matrix per forward
pass and shared by all $P$ of its weights --- that shared draw is what
couples them --- and $\varepsilon$ drawn per weight from the index-keyed
Philox stream.

$d = \mathrm{softplus}(\rho_d)$, for the same reason the mean-field arm uses
softplus. $A$ is signed, so softplus cannot bound it, and it needs a separate
learning rate instead: $10^{-5}$ for the variance group against $10^{-4}$ for
the means. One rate cannot serve parameters living at $2\times10^{-2}$ and
$10^{-4}$ at once when the optimiser's step is scale-free.

The prior is $\mathcal{N}(0, I/\lambda)$ with $\lambda = 100$, matching the
mean-field arm's prior standard deviation of $0.1$. Its KL is evaluated with
two identities so that $\Sigma$ stays factored,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{tr}(\Sigma) = \|A\|_F^2 + \|d\|^2 , \qquad
  \log\det(\Sigma) = \log\det(M) + \textstyle\sum_i \log d_i^2 , \qquad
  M = I_r + A^{\top} D^{-1} A ,
\end{equation}
which turns a $P \times P$ determinant into an $r \times r$ one. The
gradients follow from the Woodbury identity and reduce to the mean-field
gradients at $A = 0$, where the stationary point of the diagonal is
$1/\sqrt{\lambda}$, the prior standard deviation.

The posterior covers the four GEMM weight matrices in each block ---
$W_{qkv}$, $W_{proj}$, $W_{fc}$, $W_{fc2}$, or $10{,}616{,}832$ of the model's
$11{,}061{,}531$ parameters. Embeddings, LayerNorm gains and biases, and all
biases stay deterministic, and each matrix has its own $A$, $d$ and $z$, so
there is no correlation across matrices or across blocks.

Sampling is a separate kernel, not a fused GEMM prologue. The network calls
cuBLAS, so there is no GEMM of ours to fuse into, and fusing only this arm
would make the comparison measure kernel engineering instead of posterior
structure. Fusion, if done, belongs in every arm at once.

The rank is a compile-time parameter so the length-$r$ loops unroll into
registers, which means a checkpoint has to be evaluated at the rank it was
trained at. The checkpoint loader's element-count check catches a mismatch
rather than reading the wrong tensor.

\subsection{Deep ensemble (control)}
\label{sec:m:ensemble}
% The control arm: no posterior machinery at all, E independently trained
% members. Say why it is the reference the other three are measured against.

$E = 5$ independently trained deterministic models
\citep{lakshminarayanan2017simple}, differing only in the seed that
initialises their weights. The network has no dropout and the dataloader is
sequential, so initialisation is the only source of diversity; there is no
bagging, which would shrink each member's effective training set.
Architecture, data order, optimiser, learning rate, step count, batch size
and precision are identical across members and identical to the other three
arms. At inference the members' probabilities are averaged, never their
logits.

This arm introduces no kernels at all. Members train through the shared
forward and backward pass and the shared AdamW, and its evaluation callback
returns a pointer to member $s$ without sampling anything. That is exactly
why it is the reference: it is the same network with the Bayesian machinery
deleted, so every buffer and every kernel pass the other three add is
attributable to their posterior and nothing else.

At evaluation the advantage reverses. A Bayesian arm buys its $(S+1)$-th
sample for one forward pass; this arm's $S$ is capped at the number of
members it paid to train, and raising it costs another full training run.
Evaluating at $S = E$ costs every arm the same forward passes. Getting there
does not, and the harness prints the $S$ it used rather than quietly
evaluating the arms at different sample counts.
