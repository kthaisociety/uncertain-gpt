\begin{abstract}
% The mechanism claim, the measurement that carries it, and the caveat that
% keeps it honest. The controlled implementation is what makes the cost
% separable, not a contribution in itself. Every number here is a cell in a
% generated table or a constant from the source.
What a Bayesian posterior costs per training step, on a GPU, is the bytes its
optimiser moves over parameter-sized buffers, and that quantity does not depend on
how many tokens the step processes. Bayes-by-Backprop, Vadam, a learned
low-rank-plus-diagonal Gaussian covariance and a five-member deep ensemble are
implemented as four optimisation strategies over one CUDA GPT-2 of $11{,}061{,}531$
parameters, sharing kernels, batches, optimiser constants and precision, so an arm
differs only in what it stores and how many passes it makes over that store. Timed
region by region with CUDA events across a $256$-fold batch sweep at fixed sequence
length, each arm's posterior work holds to within $1.5\%$ of itself while tokens per
step go from $64$ to $16{,}384$. Its share of the step therefore falls towards
$1/B$, at a local exponent of $1.005$ for Bayes-by-Backprop at $B = 256$, but not
below $B \approx 16$, where the network's own forward and backward pass is
launch-bound rather than token-bound: $1/B$ is the asymptote and not the curve.
Folding each arm's parameter-sized passes into one kernel cuts Bayes-by-Backprop's
$O(P)$ time by $1.76\times$ and leaves Vadam's inside measurement drift, which its
pass count predicts: $11$ buffer touches per step against a floor of $9$. Evaluation
at $S = 32$ is byte-identical across that change on all four arms, and the fused
kernels are bit-identical to the ones they replace with multiply-add contraction
disabled.
\end{abstract}
